Python Superstore — Data Cleaning & Exploratory Data Analysis

An end-to-end analysis of the Sample Superstore dataset using Python. The project covers data quality assessment, cleaning, feature engineering, statistical exploration, business KPI analysis, and visual investigation of sales and profitability.

Python Pandas NumPy Matplotlib Seaborn Google Colab
Open Google Colab Notebook →

Overview

Project Overview

Objective

Prepare the raw Superstore data for reliable analysis, explore performance across products, regions, customer segments, and time, and identify patterns related to profitability, discounting, and shipping.

Dataset

Sample Superstore 2019 workbook containing Orders, Returns, and People sheets. The Orders sheet started with 9,994 rows; the cleaned analytical dataset contains 9,993 rows after removing one duplicate transaction record.

Workflow

Analysis Process

01

Data Inspection & Quality Checks

Reviewed dataset dimensions, data types, missing values, category consistency, and duplicate records. Investigated the missing postal codes and the duplicate transaction before deciding how to handle them.

02

Data Cleaning & Preparation

Standardized column names and text values, handled 11 missing postal codes as Unknown, removed one duplicate business record, and converted fields to appropriate data types.

03

Outlier Investigation

Used the IQR method to flag unusual Sales, Quantity, Discount, and Profit values. Kept flagged observations in the dataset and investigated extreme gains and losses rather than automatically deleting them.

04

Feature Engineering & Data Integration

Created profit margin, shipping duration, year/month/quarter fields, sales and discount bands, and loss indicators. Integrated return information and regional manager data.

05

Optimization & Validation

Downcast numeric types and converted suitable text columns to categorical types, reducing DataFrame memory usage from 10.18 MB to 2.07 MB while checking that Sales and Profit totals remained unchanged by optimization.

06

Exploratory & Business Analysis

Calculated business KPIs, grouped performance by category, sub-category, region, and discount band, examined yearly and monthly trends, and explored distributions and correlations.

Business Findings

Selected Insights

Category Profitability

Technology generated the highest category profit ($145,454.95) and a 17.40% margin. Furniture had a much lower 2.49% margin despite generating $741,718.42 in sales.

Regional Performance

The West recorded the highest regional sales ($725,457.82) and profit ($108,418.45). Central had the lowest regional margin at 7.92% and the highest loss-making line rate at 31.90%.

Discount & Profitability

Orders in the no-discount band generated a 29.51% margin. The 20–40% discount band had a -15.31% margin, while the above-40% band had a -77.40% margin.

Sub-Category Performance

Copiers and Paper recorded strong margins of 37.20% and 43.39%, respectively. Tables had a -8.56% margin, while Bookcases and Supplies also recorded negative profit.

Growth Over Time

Sales increased from $483,966.13 in 2016 to $733,215.26 in 2019, representing a 14.85% sales CAGR over the period. November 2019 was the strongest sales month in the dataset.

Shipping & Returns

Average shipping duration was 3.96 days. The order-level return rate was 5.91%, based on 296 distinct returned orders out of 5,009 orders.

Interpretation

Notes & Analytical Considerations

  • Outliers were flagged for investigation, not automatically removed, to preserve potentially meaningful high-value sales and profit/loss transactions.
  • The monthly linear trend had a relatively low R² (0.251 for Sales), so it should be interpreted as a broad directional trend rather than a strong predictive model.
  • Correlation results describe associations and do not establish causation. Profit margin is a derived measure (Profit divided by Sales), which should be considered when interpreting correlations.
  • The Python cleaning workflow removed one duplicate transaction with matching business fields but a different Row_ID. The SQL warehouse project retains the original 9,994 source rows, so the two projects have slightly different totals because of this documented data-cleaning decision.
